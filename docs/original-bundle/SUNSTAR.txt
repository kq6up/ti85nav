SUN AND STAR -- BACKUP ALMANAC FOR THE TI-85
============================================
Two programs that stand in for the Nautical Almanac daily pages when the
book is wet, lost or out of date. Sun and stars only. No Moon, no planets.

    SUN.ASC    V1.0   f2a37d127afebf1b3f058df97644656f
    STAR.ASC   V1.0   613ba1a4941049298c195079b6fd610b

Convert and transfer exactly like the others (see TRANSFER.txt).


WHAT THEY GIVE YOU
------------------
SUN     GHA, Dec and semidiameter at the instant you enter, then the
        equation of time on a second screen.

STAR    GHA Aries, SHA and Dec of the star on the first screen, then
        GHA of the star (Aries + SHA) on a second screen.

Both ask for the same two things, one entry each:

    DATE YYYY.MMDD      11 October 2026  ->  2026.1011
    UT HH.MMSS          16h 15m 53s      ->  16.1553

Enter the exact UT of the sight. The answer is for that instant, so there
are no increments and no v or d corrections to apply afterwards.

STAR first asks for the star number. Enter 0 to page through the list on
the calculator; ENTER moves to the next page. The name is shown as soon as
you pick a number, so a wrong number is caught before you type the date.


READING THE OUTPUT
------------------
Angles are DD.MMm, the same as the rest of the suite.

    GHA DDD.MMm   63.449     =  63 deg 44.9'
    DEC N+ S-     -7.099     =  S 7 deg 09.9'
    SD MINUTES    16         =  16.0'

The calculator drops trailing zeros. 258.3 is 258 deg 30.0', and 258.03
is 258 deg 03.0'. Read the digits after the point as MMm, padding with
zeros on the right.

    EQ TIME MM.SS   13.17    =  13 min 17 s

Positive means the Sun is ahead of the clock (it crosses the meridian
before 12:00 local mean time). The almanac prints the same number without
a sign.


USING THE RESULTS
-----------------
HO229   Take LHA = GHA -/+ your assumed longitude as on the form and use
        the displayed Dec as the total declination. For a star use the
        GHA STAR screen.

SIGHT   Give it the displayed values as if they were the tabulated ones,
        with zero time past the hour and zero v and d, since the values
        are already for the exact time of the sight.

Sun HP is not shown. It is 0.15' all year (0.1' in the tables).


HOW GOOD IS IT
--------------
Checked by running the .ASC sources line by line in 14-digit decimal
arithmetic and comparing with PyEphem apparent places.

    SUN   3000 random instants, 2024 to 2050
          GHA  worst 0.25'  typical 0.1'
          Dec  worst 0.10'
          SD   within 0.05'      Eq. of time within 2 s

    STAR  120 random instants per star, 2026 to 2036
          GHA Aries          worst 0.03'
          57 stars           worst 0.04'
          Polaris            worst 0.08'

    The 57 stars stay within 0.05' through 2045. Polaris drifts: 0.2' by
    2040 and 0.45' by 2045.

    Known case reproduced: Sun 2026-08-29 16:15:53 UT gives 63.449 and
    9.109 (63 44.9', N 9 10.9').

NOT checked: neither program has been run on a TI-85 or the emulator, and
the star table has not been compared against a printed almanac page. Run
both against a few lines of the current almanac before relying on them.


HOW IT WORKS
------------
SUN is a formula, not a table: mean longitude, mean anomaly, three-term
equation of centre, five small perturbation terms (Venus, Jupiter, Moon),
nutation and aberration, then obliquity to get Dec and the equation of
time. Good for 1901 to 2099; the date routine fails outside that.

STAR is a table: mean SHA and Dec of each star at 2030.0 with a yearly
rate for each, extrapolated in a straight line, then corrected for
aberration and nutation. The rates include proper motion.

GHA Aries is 280.46061837 + 360.98564736629 x (days from J2000), plus the
nutation term.

A fixed 69 s is used for the difference between UT and ephemeris time.


THINGS TO KNOW
--------------
Variables. Both programs use two-letter variable names only, so nothing
SETUP stores for SIGHT is disturbed. The results stay behind in GH (GHA),
DC (Dec), SD, EM (eq. of time), GA (GHA Aries) and SA (SHA).

New to this suite, so the first things to suspect if the calculator
objects: two-letter variable names, the \tan^-1\ escape (SUN only), and
labels with a digit in them such as A1 (STAR only). No equality tests, no
negation key, no ^, and no line is longer than 58 characters.

Star numbers are the almanac's own (1 Alpheratz to 57 Markab). 58 is
Polaris, added here.
